Today's News English Friday, 20 November 2026

World's top court finds Myanmar guilty of genocide against Rohingya

Source: UN News, via Google News Top Stories · Date: 19–20 November 2026 · Level: B1–B2 · Reading time: about 6 minutes

New every morning · only this page changes daily · one global hottest story, no categories

Read aloud Tap Listen on any paragraph to hear just that paragraph — never the whole lesson at once.

What is this story about?

Myanmar is a country in Asia. The Rohingya are a Muslim minority group who live there, in a state called Rakhine. In 2017, Myanmar's military attacked Rohingya villages. About 10,000 Rohingya were killed, and about 700,000 people fled across the border to Bangladesh. On Thursday 19 November 2026, the world's top court — the International Court of Justice (ICJ) in The Hague, in the Netherlands — said Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya. It is the first time the court has ever said a whole country committed genocide. The court also ordered Myanmar to repair the harm, to let Rohingya families return home safely, and to pay compensation.

News excerpt

“At the end of a three-hour ruling at the International Court of Justice (ICJ) in The Hague, President Yuji Iwasawa of Japan also declared that Myanmar had failed to prevent and punish genocide, after detailing how Myanmar's military had perpetrated ‘extreme violence’ against mainly Muslim Rohingya communities in Rakhine state. UN Secretary-General António Guterres welcomed the judgment as a ‘powerful recognition of the Rohingya victims of genocide.’”
— UN News

Key vocabulary

genocide
/ˈdʒenəsaɪd/ · noun

The crime of trying to destroy a whole group of people. “The court said Myanmar committed genocide.”

ruling
/ˈruːlɪŋ/ · noun

An official decision made by a court. “The ruling took three hours to read.”

minority
/maɪˈnɒrəti/ · noun

A small group of people inside a larger group. “The Rohingya are a Muslim minority.”

verdict
/ˈvɜːdɪkt/ · noun

A court's decision about who is right. “The verdict was historic.”

impunity
/ɪmˈpjuːnəti/ · noun

Not being punished for a crime. “Impunity must not stand.”

reparation
/ˌrepəˈreɪʃn/ · noun

Payment or action to repair harm that was done. “The court ordered reparations.”

refugee
/ˌrefjuˈdʒiː/ · noun

A person who flees their home to escape danger. “Many Rohingya are refugees in Bangladesh.”

flee
/fliː/ · verb

To run away quickly from danger. “About 700,000 people fled to Bangladesh.”

Key grammar

Find + object + adjective — “The court found Myanmar guilty.”

“Find” here means “decide after looking at the facts.” The pattern is find + someone + adjective: “The court found Myanmar guilty.” “The court found the claim true.” The adjective says what the court decided.

Passive voice — “was found guilty.”

News often uses the passive when the action matters more than who did it: be + past participle. “Myanmar was found guilty” — the important news is the verdict, not who read it out.

“The first time” + present perfect — “the first time the court has said…”

After “the first time,” English uses the present perfect (has + past participle) for something that never happened before now: “It is the first time the court has found a country guilty of genocide.”

Paragraph structure

  1. Headline — the whole story in one line. “Finds … guilty” tells you the court's decision at once.
  2. Lead (first paragraph) — who (the world's top court), what (found Myanmar guilty of genocide), and why it matters (a first in the court's history).
  3. Detail — the numbers: about 10,000 killed, about 700,000 fled, a three-hour ruling, a case brought in 2019. Numbers make the scale real.
  4. Background — the 2017 military operations in Rakhine state, and the case brought by The Gambia in 2019.
  5. What happens next — Myanmar must repair the harm, let families return home safely, pay compensation, and report back to the court within two years.

How to read a news article

  1. Read the headline first — it is the story in one sentence.
  2. Read the first paragraph. It answers: who, what, when, where, why.
  3. Look for numbers. They tell you how big, how far, how long.
  4. Look for quotes — words in “…” are what a real person said.
  5. Do not worry about every word. Find the main idea of each paragraph, then go back for the words you need.

Try it yourself

1. Which court made this ruling, and where is it?
2. What did the court order Myanmar to do?
3. In your own words, why is this ruling historic?

Answers: 1. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) in The Hague, in the Netherlands. 2. To repair the harm, let Rohingya families return home safely, and pay compensation. 3. Your answer — for example: “Because it is the first time the court has said a whole country committed genocide.”
